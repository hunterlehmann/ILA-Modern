Example 1.7 The Euclidean plane.

When \(n=2\text{,}\) we can think of \(\R^2\) as the \(xy\)-plane. We can do so because every point on the plane can be represented by an ordered pair of real numbers, namely, its \(x\)- and \(y\)-coordinates.
